Yes. Canonicalization and URL normalization are important, but they won't protect the core value of your product.

I'd focus on these 5 areas:

Keep your feed intelligence server-side

Image extraction logic
Deduplication algorithms
Feed scoring/ranking
Reliability metrics
Content enrichment Never send these rules to the browser.

Build a proprietary enrichment layer

Custom thumbnail recovery
Feed health scoring
Content categorization
Duplicate detection
Publisher trust scores Competitors can copy a UI. They cannot easily copy years of feed intelligence.

Cache and serve your own processed data

Don't expose raw parser outputs.
Serve enriched articles from your API.
Keep intermediate processing hidden.

Protect APIs aggressively

Rate limiting
API authentication
Request fingerprinting
Bot detection
Abuse monitoring This prevents people from treating your product as a free feed-extraction service.

Create a continuously improving extraction engine

New image patterns
New feed formats
Better fallback strategies
Publisher-specific quirks A competitor can inspect your site today, but they cannot inspect the improvements you'll make every week.

My biggest observation from what you've described: your moat is not the RSS reader UI. It's becoming the system that can reliably say, "If an image exists anywhere, Feedometer will find it." That is difficult to replicate and gets stronger as you process more feeds.
